What are the risk factors for Alzheimer's Disease?

Alzheimer’s disease is thought to be caused by a complex interaction of genetics, biology, and environmental factors. Risk factors increase the chance of developing it but do not guarantee development.p

Health and lifestyle factors that may affect risk of Alzheimer's disease

How old are you?

  • Most people who develop Alzheimer's disease are 65 years old or older.
  • Risk of Alzheimer's rises with age after 65.
  • Studies from the National Institute on Aging state that 'about one in 13 people age 65 to 84 and one in three people 85 and older are living with Alzheimer’s.

What's your lifestyle?

  • There are some risk factors that you can't change like age or family history but the following factors are under your control:
    1. Unhealthy Diet
    2. Smoking
    3. Alcohol Misuse
    4. Unmanaged chronic health conditions, such as high blood pressure
    5. Lack of mental stimulation
  • Researches cannot say for certain that making these changes will prevent dementia or Alzheimer's but these habits will support your overall health.

What Medical Conditions do you have?

  • Having certain medical conditions like cardiovascular disease have been linked to higher risk of developing Alzheimer's
  • For example, studies have shown that managing blood pressure reduces the risk of cognitive impairment and dementia
  • Other medical conditions associated with a higher risk of Alzheimer's include: hearing loss, mild cognitive impairment, traumatic brain injuries like a concussion, and anxiety/depression

Key Lifestyle and Health Habits that may reduce risk

Here are five ways to reduce the risk for Alzheimer's. These strategies may help reduce risk but cannot guarantee prevention.

  1. Stay physically active
    • Regular physical activity is associated with a reduced risk of dementia and supports overall brain health.
    • For adults, it is recommended to get 150 minutes of physical activity weekly
  2. Prevent or manage diabetes
    • When diabetes is not well managed, long-term damage to blood vessels can increase the risk of cognitive decline and dementia/li>
  3. Manage your blood pressure
    • High blood pressure can damage blood vessels, ultimately leading to limited blood flow to the brain
    • It also increased risk of having a stroke increases, which can damage the brain
  4. Prevent or correct hearing loss
    • Hearing loss is also closely linked to dementia
    • Research shows that untreated hearing loss is associated with an increased risk of dementia, possibly because the brain must work harder to process sound and because of reduced social engagement.
  5. Excessive alcohol use can increase the risk of high blood pressure and brain injury. Smoking significantly increases the risk of developing dementia, including Alzheimer’s disease.
    • Over time, excess drinking can lead to high blood pressure or brain injuries
    • Smoking also increases the risk of developing dementia, including Alzheimer's disease
