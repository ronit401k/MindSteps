Information on Alzheimer's Disease

What is Alzheimer's Disease

  • Alzheimer's is a progressive brain disorder that damages the brain, leading to issues in memory, thinking, cognitive capabilities, and eventually in performing simple tasks, like eating, drinking, and walking.
  • Alzheimer's is the most common form of dementia - a general term for brain diseases that cause a decline in brain function

What is Alzheimer's impact on the brain?

  • Alzheimer’s first damages the parts of the brain involved in memory. It later affects areas responsible for language, reasoning, and social behavior. Eventually, the disease damages many other areas of the brain.
  • A hallmark feature of Alzheimer’s is the buildup of abnormal clumps of proteins in the brain. These clumps, called amyloid plaques and tau tangles, disrupt the usual functions of brain cells.
  • The loss of connections between nerve cells (neurons) is another change that happens with the disease. Neurons send messages between different parts of the brain, and from the brain to muscles and organs in the body.
  • With Alzheimer’s, many neurons stop functioning properly, lose connections with other neurons, and eventually die. Many other complex brain changes are thought to play a role in the disease as well.

Source: National Institute on Aging

10 Warning Signs of Alzheimer’s Disease

  1. Memory loss that disrupts daily life
  2. Challenges in planning or solving problems
  3. Difficulty completing familiar tasks
  4. Confusion with time or place
  5. Trouble understanding visual images and spatial relationships
  6. New problems with words in speaking or writing
  7. Misplacing things and losing the ability to retrace steps
  8. Decreased judgment
  9. Withdrawal from work or social activities
  10. Changes in mood and personality

Source: Alzheimer’s Association
